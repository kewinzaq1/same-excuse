CAVEMAN

The caveman in your head is not the enemy. He is a fast learner.

He does whatever was easiest and most rewarded last time. Caveman is a creature in your phone that grows only when you do one small real thing in your life. You do not fight him. You train him, one two-minute action at a time.

The method

Four moves. Each one has a study behind it. Watch him grow while you read.

stage 1 · just hatched
MOVE ONE

Plan the when and where, not the what.

Goals are wishes. Plans are cues. You tell Caveman the exact moment and place, in your own words, and that is the only notification you ever get.

If it is 7:00 and I am in the kitchen, then I open the laptop and write one sentence. If-then plans raise goal attainment by d = 0.65 across 94 studies. Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006.
MOVE TWO

Make it so small it is embarrassing.

Two push-ups. One sentence. One message sent. The action has to survive your worst day, because the caveman only learns from repetition, never from intensity. It grows on its own after seven clean days.

Automaticity comes from repeated cue-action pairs; a median of 66 days. Lally et al., 2010. Behavior = motivation × ability × prompt. Fogg, 2009.
MOVE THREE

Do it, prove it, and let the feeling follow.

Do not wait for motivation. Your steps, workouts and sleep come from your phone's sensors. A page you wrote is a photo. No forms. Then the creature reacts, and that is your reward, small and a little surprising every time.

Acting before you feel like it matched full therapy for depression in a 440-person trial. Richards et al., 2016. Expected rewards lower motivation; surprise ones do not. Deci, Koestner & Ryan, 1999.
MOVE FOUR

Every action is a vote for who you are becoming.

Caveman does not log tasks cleared. It logs evidence. Day 12 as a person who ships. Day 30 as a person who moves. You infer who you are from what you did, and now there is a creature wearing the proof.

People read their identity off their own behavior. Bem, 1972. Mastery experiences are the strongest source of self-belief. Bandura, 1977.
Your caveman

He never dies, never guilt-trips, and never sends you "we miss you".

A miss is a data point. He waits, visibly, and is glad when you come back. The research is blunt about this: people who are treated kindly after a failure try harder next time, not less.

Legs from moving

Walks, runs, gym. Read straight from your phone's health data. His legs get longer.

Fire from building

A page written, a lesson finished, a commit pushed. Snap a photo. He carries the torch.

A crown from reaching out

One message to someone you have been meaning to write. Weak ties are where jobs and ideas come from.

What it is not

Most of the category is built to keep you in the app. This is built to get you out of it.

Not a feed

There is nothing to scroll. Under four minutes a day is the target, and above it we treat the product as broken.

Not a points economy

You cannot buy progress and you cannot lose it. Paying people for what they already enjoy makes them enjoy it less.

Not a meditation app

No journal, no mood tracker, no breathing circle. Real actions in the real world, verified where possible.

Not a guru

No quotes on sunsets. Every claim on this page names its study, and the ones that failed replication are listed too.
